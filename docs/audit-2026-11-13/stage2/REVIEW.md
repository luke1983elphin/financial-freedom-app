# Second-stage financial accuracy review — Draft PR #61

Review date: 13 November 2026 UTC. Starting revision: `065de0410698bb82edb66a4f192ed765e0dd102c`. Financial source is unchanged from main `6f3dbf83cc744e765eddf6244313635ea357bc9a`. This supplements, and where stated corrects, the [first-stage report](../REPORT.md); that historical report and its evidence have not been rewritten.

**NO-GO for implementing a user-facing Monte Carlo engine on the present financial model.** A research specification can proceed. Financial-engine corrections need explicit approval in separate PRs before implementation. Nothing in this review authorises financial source, protected-hash, golden-fixture, migration, merge or deployment changes.

The most consequential confirmed error remains F08: the detailed engine computes tax and then discards a person's negative cash result before combining household cash. F04 is real but its scope needs refinement. F07 includes both wrong starting assets and wrong returns, including in the plan-linked adapter. A newly confirmed **F11 duplicates property across canonical and legacy representations** in detailed retirement. These are deterministic conservation/reconciliation problems that a stochastic wrapper cannot repair.

F01 is primarily an unresolved contract/disclosure issue; F02 a convention and cross-engine consistency issue; F03 a conditional overlap where the same income is represented twice, not a prohibition on separate investment income. F05/F06 are demonstrable unsupported tax treatments, rather than errors in every supported ordinary-income example. F09 contains approximation and eligibility limitations, not evidence that the current pension constants are wrong. F10 is a domain restriction that blocks realistic loss simulations, rather than a failed promise to simulate negative explicit returns today.

### F08 root cause and financial conservation

[semiRetirementProjection.js:2111](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2111) assigns `max(0, cashBeforeTax − incomeTax − Medicare − MLS − HELP)` to `person.netEmploymentIncome`; the pension recalculation repeats it at [2260](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2260). Despite the name, `cashBeforeTax` includes passive cash. Clamping each owner before household aggregation means an owner with taxable reinvestment but no distribution contributes zero cash rather than negative tax cash. `totalNetEmploymentIncome` and `netHouseholdCashIncome` at [2265](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2265) therefore omit the obligation. The funding waterfall near [2394](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2394) and year-end settlement near [2510](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2510) never receive it. Account-local reconciliation can still report zero difference because it reconciles an already incomplete movement set.

Independent no-return funding acceptance is unambiguous: $100k opening accessible assets, $60k taxable income, zero cash receipts/spending and $9,620 tax must leave $90,380, or an explicit equivalent liability if borrowing is chosen. The present result is $100k. At $5k cash, close at $95,380. At $20k cash, no portfolio tax draw is needed, and the remaining $10,380 follows the selected surplus destination. The chosen scenario must specify the economic source of noncash taxable income; these isolators test the tax movement, while the $2m/3%-yield holding supplies a realistic retained-income source.

With a $100k working spouse, ordinary tax is $22,520. Household net after the other owner's $9,620 is $67,860, not $77,480. The correction must aggregate signed person cash first; it must not always force a sale in the income owner's account when spouse cash covers the liability.

Accumulation is different: [calculator.js:2661](../../../calculator.js#L2661) retains signed current after-tax cash correctly. [projectConfiguredInvestments:1799](../../../calculator.js#L1799) floors affordable contributions at zero but does not fund negative `remainingSurplus` from existing assets or carry a liability. The $2m original H41 wealth gap of $10,277.58 uses a specified year-end settlement and monthly yield convention. It is not a uniquely determined corrected close under all settlement timings or CGT strategies. A future fix must decide between a fixed-budget illustration with an explicit unfunded debt and an integrated lifetime funding model.

Weekly planning uses [estimatedNetIncomeAnnuals:543](../../../weekly-plan.js#L543), `buildDefaultTimingItems:590` and `createFromPlan`, relying on estimated payroll net and other cash income. In the no-cash $2m investment case, main current net is **−$9,620** but weekly receipts and essential costs are both zero; the tax is not scheduled. This is an investment-tax/calendar reconciliation gap, not proof payroll withholding itself is wrong. Due-date planning and annual tax settlement are intentionally different concepts and need an explicit bridge.

### F04/F11 root cause and isolation

[calculator.js:2866](../../../calculator.js#L2866) `netWorthProjection` adds gross property value but its closing-balance subtraction omits rental-property loans. Current `totalLiabilities` includes the debt. [app.js:1693](../../../app.js#L1693) `projectedDebtAtYear` separately includes home-loan, HELP, other-debt and credit-card values, omitting rental loans. It supplies dashboard/report debt charts and report outlook/decision comparisons. `applyRetirementForecast` replaces net-worth rows but does **not** replace this app helper. Thus fixing `calculator.js` alone is insufficient.

F11 arises in [semiRetirementUi.js:388](../../../semiRetirementUi.js#L388) `projectionAssetsFromPlan`. Its `hasLegacyOtherProperty` predicate near [421](../../../semiRetirementUi.js#L421) recognises `other-property` or a legacy ID, but an existing `investment-property` group does not satisfy it. `syncCollectionsToLegacy` at [app.js:2293](../../../app.js#L2293) legitimately mirrors canonical properties to `assets.otherPropertyValue`; the retirement adapter then treats that nonauthoritative mirror as an additional asset. [semiRetirementProjection.js:2190](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2190) totals both nonaccessible values and correctly subtracts scheduled debt at [2560](../../../semiRetirementProjection.js#L2560). The defect is asset mapping, not failure of the detailed debt subtraction.

Control: use one `otherProperty` record with an explicit 3% growth override. The detailed result is $268k, debt $350k, and no duplication. The main report debt helper still shows $0. Without an override, generic `otherProperty` intentionally has a detailed 0% fallback (`propertyTypeGroup`, `resolvePlanPropertyGrowthRatePct`, lines 219/272), while main accumulation applies its generic investment-property growth. That scope difference can create another $18k/year-one discrepancy; it must be disclosed/aligned by decision, not misdiagnosed as omitted debt. Simply changing category to avoid F11 is not a safe user-data migration.

### F07 root cause

[calculator.js:1831](../../../calculator.js#L1831) `rowAtAge` selects the first end-year age at or after the requested age. At current retirement age, there is no time-zero row, so [2826–2838](../../../calculator.js#L2826) uses a future close as the starting pot. It then credits `expectedInvestmentReturn` to super in the combined-pot weighting. [semiRetirementUi.js:889](../../../semiRetirementUi.js#L889) independently repeats both problems when re-creating the simple illustrations after the detailed forecast replaces plan rows: first matching **closing** FI wealth as starting capital, and the non-super return for the whole pot.

[simulateRetirement:1849](../../../calculator.js#L1849) also always initialises a checkpoint named `60` with starting assets, regardless of actual starting age. That field must not be presented as reconstructed age-60 wealth for an older retiree. It withdraws at the beginning of a year, unlike midpoint detailed funding; this is an additional intentional approximation once the starting balance/return errors are removed. Retiring before age 60 needs a real accessible-assets bridge, not silently moving the starting pool to age 60 while keeping an earlier label.

## Intentional differences that must not be “repaired” by forcing equality

- **Legacy fixed-budget accumulation versus plan-linked lifetime cashflow.** Wage growth, retirement phases, spending, surplus destinations and drawdowns differ. `app.js:2411` applies `applyRetirementForecast` only when saved timing is present and valid. It replaces future rows, while many current-year fields remain from the main calculator. Invalid linked inputs can fall back to legacy at the adapter boundary; comparison code guards against mixing routes within one comparison. Surface engine/provenance explicitly.
- **Annual timing.** Main accumulation adds monthly contributions before returns; detailed retirement uses midpoint movement; simple sustainability withdraws at year start. Detailed rows describe opening ages, main forecast rows end-year ages. The adapter maps completed years. Compare opening to opening and closing to closing before judging a difference. A time-zero fallback error remains an error despite these conventions.
- **Return and account phase.** Detailed new defaults use super 6.5% before retirement and 5.5% afterwards; main legacy accumulation stays at 6.5%. Reduced work/full retirement is not legal verification of an exempt pension account. Investments and super must retain separate rate/basis/account metadata.
- **Today dollars.** `dollar-basis.js` deflates nominal values only for presentation. It must not change inputs, returns, cash taxes or saved nominal outcomes. The traced nominal results stay unchanged when the display basis changes. Annual sums of real amounts must discount each year's cashflow separately.
- **Weekly planning.** A dated cash calendar with actual overrides is not an annual accrual/portfolio ledger. Annual rent can arrive as a single scheduled payment, so one week's bank balance is not annual income/52. Tax provisions and annual settlement need reconciliation rather than fabricated equality of every week to an annual average.
- **Property and accessible wealth.** Principal residence/property equity is not spendable cash without a specified sale/release event. Generic `otherProperty` and investment-property defaults have different growth scope. Explicit per-asset rates and saved retirement assumptions can intentionally override global growth comparisons.
- **Pension.** Deemed income is not actual investment income or a second cash return. Annual means tests and assumed CPI indexation approximate actual fortnightly entitlement and statutory indexation. A 4% FI capital heuristic is not the retirement spending waterfall or a probability of success.

## Australian tax and return treatment

Detailed source status is in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). Verified published guidance is separated from the app's assumptions and from unresolved specialist eligibility. No blanket tax deduction is proposed.

| Topic | Appropriate financial model contract | Current capability / decision needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investment ordinary income | Tax income attributable to each owner, whether paid as cash or reinvested; distinguish cash from tax amounts | Explicit yield recognises taxable reinvestment correctly, but F08 loses funding. Bare aggregate return has no tax-character breakdown |
| Franking | Eligible gross-up adds credits to assessable income; offset/refund is a separate settlement; preserve cash dividend amount | F05 unsupported. Residency, holding-period/related-payment restrictions and distribution character matter; don't assume all credits refundable |
| CGT | Realisation/cost base, losses before eligible discounts, ownership and dates determine taxable gain | No comprehensive sale/cost-base/loss ledger. | Super earnings tax | Distinguish accumulation and eligible exempt retirement-phase income, with fund expenses and CGT treatment; a published net return may already incorporate fund tax | No explicit fund tax engine. | Contributions tax | Ordinary concessional contributions generally taxed 15%; distinguish nonconcessional/downsize contributions | Ordinary treatment present. LISTO, caps, notice-of-intent/deductibility and age eligibility need support or an explicit exclusion. H03's 15% arithmetic pass does not establish low-income net benefit correctness |
| Division 293 | Additional tax on the lesser of relevant low-tax contributions and relevant income-plus-contributions threshold excess; payment from cash or super must be explicit | F06 unsupported. | Fees | State which investment/admin/adviser/insurance costs are embedded versus separately deducted and when paid | Explicit detailed fee percentages default zero. Already net-of-fee input plus the same separate fee would double-charge; omission is conditional on quoted-return basis |

| Pension/Medicare | Distinguish current means-test parameters, income tax/SAPTO, Medicare concessions and future policy assumptions | Current pension constants checked; family/pensioner Medicare and other specialist eligibility are not comprehensively certified. A future gross-return option needs income/capital composition and a realisation strategy. A future net-return option needs an explicit “net of what” contract. Neither can be inferred safely from 7% or 6.5% alone.

## Monte Carlo decision

**GO now:** write a research/design specification and independent deterministic acceptance ledgers; retain read-only audit diagnostics. **NO-GO now:** implement probability outputs by randomising existing scalar return fields or by clamping sampled losses to pass positive-only validation.

Minimum entry gates, each with an owner and independent evidence, are:

1. Correct F08 so every tax/expense is paid from permitted funds, carried as a liability or explicitly unfunded; include spouse pooling, fees, pension-enabled funding and zero-asset cases. Accounts must reconcile to an independent household ledger, not just their own incomplete cash movements.
2. Resolve F04/F11 and common report debt helpers. One economic asset/debt has one identity, and all intended balance-sheet surfaces reconcile at the same instant.
